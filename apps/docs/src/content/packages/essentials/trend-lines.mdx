Values go up and down. Is the series growing anyway? `trendLine()` answers with one straight line through it, dashed and in the series' color. It works on the presets and in composed charts, on bars, lines and points. For a fixed goal or limit, see [target lines](../target-lines/).

It comes from `@chartist-js/essentials`, with its stylesheet after Chartist's:

```ts
import 'chartist/styles.css';
import '@chartist-js/essentials/styles.css';
```

## A trend through bars

Presets take extra layers in their `layers` option: `above` draws them on top of the bars or lines. On bar charts, `align: 'center'` lines the trend up with the middle of the bars:

<LiveExample
  title='Bars with a trend line'
  label='Bar chart of monthly sign-ups with a dashed line rising through the bars'
  gallery='essentials/trend-line'
/>

The line runs from the series' first to its last value. Where it ends below or above the values, the Y axis grows to keep it in view. Holes are skipped, and a series with fewer than two values gets no line. Without options, every series gets a line; `series` picks some by name or index.

| Option       | What it does                                                           |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `series`     | The series to fit, by name or index; by default all of them            |
| `x`, `y`     | The ids of the axes to draw on, in a composed chart                    |
| `align`      | `'start'` (default) or `'center'` of each step, to line up with bars   |
| `classNames` | `{ series, line }` replace the classes `ct-series` and `ct-trend-line` |

## Points on a scale

The fit is least squares: on a step axis it uses the values' order, on a scale axis their `x`. So a scatter plot gets its regression line:

## How well it fits

A rising line says little when the values jump around it. Each trend line's node has the line's `slope` and `intercept`, and its `correlation`: Pearson's r, from -1 for values that all lie on a falling line, through 0 for no straight trend, to 1 for a rising one. Its square is the R² that spreadsheets show next to a trendline, the share of the ups and downs the line explains. A listener can read it from the scene:

<LiveExample
  title='Two trends, two fits'
  label='Line chart of two shops over twelve weeks with a trend line each; the text below says Basel grows 1.9 a week with an R² of 0.98, and Bern 1.5 a week with an R² of 0.26'
  example='trend-lines/fit'
  show='src/main.ts'
/>

Both shops grow, but only Basel's line describes its weeks. With two values r is always 1 or -1, so a fit needs a few values before it means much. A [tooltip](../../tooltips/content-and-styling/) can show the same numbers on hover.

## Exponential growth

On a [log axis](../log-axis/), the line is fitted to the logarithms of the values, so it's straight there: an exponential trend on a log Y axis, a power law on two log axes. Here an animal's metabolic rate grows with its mass to the power of about ¾:

<LiveExample
  title='A power law'
  label='Body mass against resting metabolic rate for seven animals from a mouse to an elephant, on two log axes, with a dashed trend line running straight through the points'
  gallery='essentials/log-log'
/>

The `slope` is then in powers of the base per step, here about 0.75: the rate grows by a factor of 10 to the 0.75 for every tenfold mass.

## Styling

A trend line takes its series' color. Its width and dash pattern are custom properties:

```css
.ct-chart {
  --ct-trend-line-width: 1px;
  --ct-trend-line-dasharray: 2px 2px;
}
```

The markup: a `line.ct-trend-line` in a series group (`g.ct-series.ct-series-a`) per line. In listeners and plugins, find them by the role `trendLine`, as in [events and plugins](../../../docs/events-and-plugins/). Importing `@chartist-js/essentials` declares the role, so TypeScript knows its context.
